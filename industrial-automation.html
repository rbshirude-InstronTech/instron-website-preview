How we work

Electrical & instrumentation

E&I design and selection, from the process up

The control system is only as reliable as the measurement and wiring behind it, so instruments and electrical design start from the process, not a vendor catalogue.

  • Instrument selectionMeasuring principle, range, accuracy, response and wetted materials, matched to process conditions, hygienic or hazardous-area needs and the decision each signal supports.
  • Instrument documentationSpecifications and data sheets, instrument and I/O lists, and the signal, power and communication details for every device.
  • Electrical designI/O and load definition, motor and VFD interfaces, cable schedules, termination details, grounding, shielding and segregation.
  • Control panelsComponent selection, layout and wire identification; UL 508A, CSA or CE approval for your market; power-up, I/O checks and functional simulation before shipment.
  • Field integrationLoop checks, rotation and actuation checks, network commissioning and as-built updates.

In practice: 26 VFDs and 1,000+ I/O on a Canadian compounding plant; NEMA 4X panels on a Toronto CIP skid; hazardous-area instrumentation on an API skid in India.

Batch & regulated production

Batch control, audit trail and pharma compliance

Recipes change without rewriting code, and every batch leaves a record quality can sign off.

  • S88 / ISA-88
  • 21 CFR Part 11
  • GAMP 5
  • IQ / OQ

S88 batch architecture

The physical model (units, equipment and control modules) kept separate from the procedural model (procedures, operations, phases).

Recipes and phases

Parameterized recipes; reusable phases for dosing, heating, cooling, agitation, transfer and CIP; consistent hold, resume, abort and restart. Interlocks stay protected while recipe parameters stay configurable within limits.

Audit trail and electronic records

User actions, recipe changes, alarms and events recorded with time and user. Batch reports carry the parameters quality needs. 21 CFR Part 11 compliant where the application requires it.

Computer system validation (CSV)

URS, functional design specification, hardware and software design specifications, FAT and SAT protocols and executed records, following a GAMP 5 approach. We execute installation and operational qualification (IQ/OQ) with your QA team.

Delivered: a 21 CFR Part 11 adjuvant & CIP skid in Toronto with ISA-88 sequencing and URS, FDS, FAT and SAT; an API skid in India with Part 11 audit trails; ISA-88 recipes and paperless audit trail on a Canadian compounding plant. See Pharma & Life Sciences and Batch & Process Automation.

Brownfield method

Modernize without stopping the plant

Keep the equipment that still works. Replace the fragile control layer in stages you can test and roll back.

  1. AssessInstalled PLCs, HMIs, panels, networks, field devices and documentation, with backups and baseline records captured first.
  2. Define the migration strategyKeep, migrate, replace or isolate, with a technical reason for each.
  3. Plan the implementationPhased cutovers, temporary interfaces, testing requirements and rollback paths around production.
  4. Simulate, test and validateSimulation, functional testing and FAT on the high-risk changes before anyone goes to site.
  5. Commission and hand overImplement the approved change, verify operation and update drawings, backups and system documentation.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Can you work with our existing PLC, HMI and SCADA systems?

Yes. We engineer on Rockwell (Allen-Bradley), Siemens, Schneider, Mitsubishi and Honeywell platforms. We review your installed equipment, software versions, backups and plant standards, then recommend what to retain, integrate or upgrade. Your coding and naming standards take precedence over ours.

Can you integrate equipment from different suppliers?

Yes. We start with the existing makes, models and software, define how each system communicates, over OPC UA or native protocols, and test the integration with the suppliers before start-up.

Can you handle just the automation scope if we already have an equipment supplier?

Yes. We work directly with plant teams or under OEMs, skid builders and EPC contractors. You can engage us for PLC and HMI programming, SCADA integration and reporting, control panel supply, commissioning support, or a combination. Responsibilities for equipment, wiring, software, testing and start-up are set before work begins.

How do you plan an automation upgrade around our production schedule?

We capture backups and a baseline of the installed system first, then define the migration strategy: what to keep, migrate, replace or isolate. Everything that can be built and tested offline is, with simulation and FAT on the high-risk changes where they are in scope. The cutover is planned into your shutdown window, staged to reduce downtime, with a way back to the previous configuration.

Can you automate our cleaning process and provide cycle records?

Yes. We automate clean-in-place (CIP) and other cleaning sequences. Cycle records can capture the steps performed, durations, alarms, outcomes and the measurements available from the system; what is recorded is defined in the scope review. For example, we automated an adjuvant and CIP skid at a global healthcare company's Toronto site, with 900+ I/O.

Do you design and supply control panels?

Yes. We provide control panels with UL 508A, CSA or CE approval, whichever your market needs, within the system scope or as a separate supply.

How do you handle machine and process safety in an automation project?

We implement operating interlocks, permissives and trips in the process control logic, based on your process-risk studies, and can support HAZOP. Where the project needs dedicated safety functions, they are kept separate from process control, with their requirements, testing and documentation defined in the scope. All safety logic is checked at FAT and SAT.

Can you support validation for pharmaceutical automation projects?

Yes. We have delivered 21 CFR Part 11 compliant systems since 2015. We produce the validation documentation following a GAMP 5 approach, including functional specifications, test protocols and test records, and execute IQ and OQ with your QA team.

Who owns the automation code, and what do we receive at handover?

Software ownership, access rights and handover deliverables are set out in the proposal. Handover can include native PLC and HMI project files, SCADA backups, revision history, drawings, manuals, test records and operator or maintenance training, so you are not dependent on one programmer. Any third-party licence terms are identified up front.

What support is available after commissioning?

Remote or on-site support for troubleshooting, punch-list items and later changes, plus change management and staged obsolescence planning. Support coverage, response times and warranty terms are agreed as part of the project.

What information do you need to prepare an initial proposal?

A short description of what you want the equipment or process to do and where you need help. If available, share process drawings, an I/O list, PLC and HMI models, software backups and any production or shutdown constraints. We review what you have and identify the details needed to confirm the scope.

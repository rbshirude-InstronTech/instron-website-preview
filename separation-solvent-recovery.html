Process applications / Separation & Solvent Recovery

Engineer the full separation and recovery loop.

Recovery and purity matter, but so do feed variation, utilities, solvent management, fouling, operator actions and safe operation.

Technical starting point

Start with feed data and the acceptance target.

Define composition, expected variability, target purity and recovery, batch or continuous throughput, and the constraints that shape the route. A laboratory result may support a separation hypothesis without yet defining a reliable recycle or production system.

Explore our engineering phases

Technical considerations

What the application has to address.

Typical scoping questions. The answers shape the scope, deliverables and acceptance criteria for your project.

01

Distillation and evaporation

Review heat and material balance, volatility or boiling behavior, reflux, duty, pressure, turndown, fouling, cleaning and utility requirements.

02

Extraction and phase handling

Assess partition behavior, selectivity, solvent ratio, contact time, settling, emulsions, interface detection and solvent recovery.

03

Recycle-loop definition

Specify recovered-solvent quality, buildup of impurities, make-up, purge, acceptable losses and compatibility with upstream production.

04

Instrumentation and safety

Define monitoring, interlocks, control of heaters/pumps/valves, materials compatibility and the responsible safety/regulatory review scope.

✳

Every project requires a defined technical owner, acceptance basis and a scope proportionate to its operating context.

Scope logic

From an engineering question to an operating system.

The aim is to distinguish what is known, what must be engineered and what evidence is needed next.

01 / APPLICATION LAYER

Process basis

Feed, balances, separation objectives and viable operating envelope.

02 / APPLICATION LAYER

Skid and controls integration

Equipment layout, P&IDs, instrumentation, control philosophy and package boundaries.

03 / APPLICATION LAYER

Acceptance evidence

FAT and commissioning plans tied to actual recovery, quality and operating behavior where test conditions permit.

Delivery sequence

A practical path toward the next decision.

  1. 01Clarify feed, purity, recovery and throughput
  2. 02Select or test the process route and operating envelope
  3. 03Develop equipment, utility and control interfaces
  4. 04Plan acceptance, commissioning and solvent handling

Contract stages remain separate. Progression from Basic Engineering & Feasibility to Detailed Engineering or Integrated Design & Build is not automatic.

First conversation

Start with the evidence already available.

A useful first discussion is about the existing basis, the technical uncertainty and the milestone it affects. A complete specification is not required to start clarifying the scope.

Prepare a technical enquiry
Useful inputs to bring
  • Feed composition and variability
  • Target product or recovered stream specifications
  • Lab or pilot operating results
  • Pressure, temperature, hazard and utility constraints

Common questions

Decisions worth clarifying.

Is every solvent recovery project a distillation project?

No. Depending on the feed and objective, separation may involve distillation, extraction, evaporation, membranes, filtration or a combination.

Can Instron Technologies help before a final technology route is chosen?

A scoped feasibility or basic engineering engagement can clarify requirements and open decisions; the separation technology itself may require specialist or technology-owner input.

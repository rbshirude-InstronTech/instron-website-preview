Our commitments

Put responsibilities into the project scope.

Engineering authority & design responsibility

Determine jurisdiction, professional review, applicable codes and sign-off needs at the start of each engagement.

Process safety and site EHS

Assign design and site safety responsibilities, operating safeguards, hazard-review inputs and escalation points.

Quality, testing & validation

Agree URS and project basis, FAT/SAT scope, document revisions and evidence required for acceptance or client qualification.

Cybersecurity & industrial data

Define access, networking, segmentation, credentials, remote support and security approvals with the customer IT/OT owners.

Privacy & confidentiality

Agree who may receive drawings, data, photos and technical records, and use contractual confidentiality controls when necessary.

Vendor due diligence

Respond to requests for entity, insurance, competencies and project assurance records through an appropriate verification process.

Certifications, licensure and regulatory approvals are confirmed for each project; project-specific evidence is provided for review.

Due diligence

What a customer should be able to verify.

1

Applicable jurisdiction

Engineering sign-off, pressure equipment, electrical and installation requirements depend on the project and location.

2

Defined acceptance

FAT, site testing, commissioning and validation boundaries are linked to an agreed scope and applicable standards.

3

Evidence package

Written requirements, specifications, drawings, tests and responsibility records are reviewed against project obligations.

Company credentials, project documentation, certifications and regulatory information are available on request, subject to applicability, verification and confidentiality.

Responsible engineering

Design choices that can be explained and measured.

Sustainability starts with a process that can be understood, measured and improved.

Resource recovery and separation

Assess process opportunities for solvent recovery, utilities and material reuse, measured against project-specific requirements.

Utility and energy visibility

Establish reliable metering, equipment state context and operating baselines before recommending efficiency changes.

Designed for maintainability

Consider service access, documentation, parts and commissioning so new equipment can be operated and supported.

Responsible project collaboration

Clarify EHS ownership, contractor interfaces, local regulations and decisions that require independent specialists.
